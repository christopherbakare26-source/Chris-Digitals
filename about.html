How I got here

I started working with WordPress in 2022, and four years later it's still the platform I choose for client work — not because it's fashionable, but because it gives a business owner real ownership of their own website.

Most of my work comes through Fiverr, Upwork and direct client relationships. That mix has taught me something important: the technical part of a project is rarely the hard part. The hard part is understanding what a business actually needs, and then delivering it without surprises.

I've built landing pages for service businesses, improved existing WordPress sites that were losing enquiries, refined layouts for educational brands, and fixed the kind of bugs that quietly cost a business customers — broken forms, layouts that collapse on a phone, pages that take too long to load.

How I approach a project

I don't have a single favourite page builder, and I think that's an advantage. Divi, Elementor, Kadence, WPBakery and the native Gutenberg editor all solve different problems. Choosing the right one for your site — rather than the one I'm most comfortable with — means you get a site that's easier to maintain and better suited to how you'll actually use it.

Before I build anything, I want to know what the website is for. Is it there to generate enquiries? Sell products? Establish credibility with people who've already heard of you? That answer shapes the layout, the copy hierarchy, and where the calls to action sit.

Then I build it, test it on desktop, tablet and phone, and hand it over with a walkthrough so you can manage it yourself. If something needs changing three months later, you shouldn't have to book a developer to change a phone number.

What I'm committed to

Reliable website solutions. That means the site loads, the forms send, the layout holds together on a small screen, and the person who answers your emails is the person who built it. No hand-offs to an anonymous team, no disappearing after delivery.

I'm also comfortable working as part of a wider team — alongside agencies, designers or other developers — where I handle the WordPress build while someone else owns the brand or the strategy.
